**Turn the wish to make an animation into a first scene you can actually build.** This book introduces the possibilities of animation, then brings the reader into a practical, image-guided workflow: prepare a character, create poses, improve motion, move the camera, reuse effects and finish with editing and sound. The main hands-on lessons use **Stick Nodes**, with an emphasis on getting started on a phone.

## A friendly route from curiosity to creation

Animation can feel distant when you only see the finished film. The strength of this guide is that it makes the next small action visible. Screenshots, marked controls and short instructions connect an idea such as a greeting gesture to the poses and frames needed to make it move. Its encouraging, conversational voice invites readers to try, play back the result and improve it.

The opening chapter offers a short introduction to animation history and styles, including 2D, 3D, clay/stop motion, drawing and anime. The practical core then focuses on a **2D stickfigure workflow**, rather than attempting to teach every animation package. That focus gives beginners a concrete place to start while showing where their new skills fit into the wider art form.

## What you will explore

1. **History and styles:** meet different approaches to animation before choosing what to make.
2. **Characters and backgrounds:** install the app, find and import assets, select a character and prepare a scene.
3. **Poses and movement:** build a greeting animation, experiment with frame spacing and timing, and use tweening to connect poses more smoothly.
4. **Creative controls:** bring in custom images, build figures from simple shapes, adjust the camera, move several characters together and change scene framing.
5. **Effects and scene management:** import effects, create reusable animated elements, change size, angle and colour, duplicate figures, arrange their depth, lock them and capture the result.
6. **Editing and sound:** follow a short closing introduction to bringing the animation into an editor and adding music or other audio.

## Why this book belongs in the portfolio

- **Visual teaching with a clear purpose.** The illustrations are part of the lesson: they show which control or joint to use, helping readers connect the written explanation to the app.
- **Small exercises with room for imagination.** A waving character is an approachable first result; later prompts ask readers to combine characters and actions into a little scene of their own.
- **A route beyond downloaded assets.** Custom images, simple figure construction and reusable effects encourage readers to adapt a scene rather than rely only on ready-made characters.
- **Attention to the whole scene.** Camera movement, framing, simultaneous motion, layering and sound help readers think about how a sequence is presented, as well as how a figure moves.
- **An accessible starting point.** The phone-focused practical lessons make the project relevant to learners who want to begin creating with the equipment already in their hands.

For Bardiya and Armiya, this project also brings together animation, teaching and publishing: turning creative experience into an illustrated learning resource that other people can follow.

## Who it is for

Curious beginners, students, aspiring animators and creators who want to make their first short 2D scenes. It is particularly suited to readers who learn by seeing an example and trying it themselves.
